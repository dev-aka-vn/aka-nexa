# AI-SPEC — Phase 3: Natural-Language Routing

> AI design contract generated by `/gsd-ai-integration-phase`. Consumed by `gsd-planner` and `gsd-eval-auditor`.
> Locks framework selection, implementation guidance, and evaluation strategy before planning begins.

## 1. System Classification

**System Type:** Bounded natural-language intent classification and authorized tool selection (Extraction; not an autonomous agent, RAG system or chatbot).

**Description:** Employees describe a task in Slack; the platform resolves identity, filters the app catalogue by RBAC, and a versioned decision provider selects an authorized app or abstains. The same signed Phase 2 form link is issued only for a sufficiently confident, re-authorized choice. A rule-based provider works entirely offline and is the initial production baseline; hosted providers are optional and disabled until approved. English and Vietnamese (including mixed-language requests) receive equal core support.

**Critical Failure Modes:**
1. Exposing an unauthorized app in a provider payload, clarification choice, cache hit, or issued form link.
2. Issuing a wrong app's form rather than clarifying on weak, conflicting, unknown, malformed or unsupported-language requests.
3. Blocking chat routing when a hosted provider is slow/unavailable, or passing an expired shared deadline to the local fallback.
4. Sending identifiable text to a hosted provider without regional approval, redaction, and verified no-training/no-retention terms; leaking text via logs or traces.
5. Reusing a cached single-turn choice for another conversation context or bypassing a fresh permission check on a follow-up.

## 1b. Domain Context

> Researched by `gsd-domain-researcher`. Grounds the evaluation strategy in domain expert knowledge.

**Industry Vertical:** Enterprise employee-service workflows: HR/leave, IT/ticketing, procurement and other department-owned internal apps; this is task routing, not medical or legal advice and not a general chatbot.

**User Population:** Employees submitting English, Vietnamese or mixed-language requests in Slack; department app owners and administrators who publish and govern those apps.

**Stakes Level:** High overall; unauthorized app disclosure or cross-department access is Critical.

**Output Consequence:** A clear, authorized choice yields the Phase 2 signed link to that app's form; the employee's later submission may become an HR record, IT ticket or procurement request. An incorrect choice sends the employee to the wrong workflow; an unauthorized choice or exposed app name reveals a restricted capability. The decision provider does not itself execute the downstream action.

### What Domain Experts Evaluate Against

**Dimension: Correct service desk destination**
- **Good (domain expert would accept):** “I want to request leave” opens the authorized, published leave-request form just as `/leave request` would; “my laptop won't connect” is offered the applicable IT incident app, not a laptop purchase app, when those apps are published and available to this employee.
- **Bad (domain expert would flag):** The system routes an IT incident to procurement merely because both descriptions mention “laptop,” or treats “I need an out-of-office message” as a leave request without resolving the overlap.
- **Stakes:** High.
- **Source:** `.planning/ROADMAP.md` Phase 3 success criterion 1; `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` RTE-01/RTE-02 and APP-04; Qian et al., *Distinguish Sense from Nonsense* (EMNLP Industry 2022), on semantically overlapping enterprise intents.

**Dimension: Department boundary and current permission**
- **Good (domain expert would accept):** For an employee authorized for an IT ticket but not an HR leave app, only the IT app can appear in the decision input, numbered choices and signed link; if access changes between a first message and a clarification reply, the now-revoked app disappears even if it was previously offered. A person's name in the message (including a homonym) never substitutes for the authenticated requester.
- **Bad (domain expert would flag):** A previously cached choice, quoted app name, or stale same-thread reply reveals or opens an HR app after revocation, or access to one department's app is mistaken for access to another's.
- **Stakes:** Critical.
- **Source:** `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` ACL-01/ACL-06/ACL-07/RTE-03; `03-CONTEXT.md` D-48/D-50; OWASP LLM06:2025 Excessive Agency (least privilege and complete mediation).

**Dimension: Sensible question instead of the wrong form**
- **Good (domain expert would accept):** An unsupported, vague or conflicting request receives a concise question naming no more than three currently authorized app choices; “1” or a displayed app name in the same thread resolves the choice, while extra details are interpreted together with the original request. After two unanswered clarifications, offer authorized examples or slash commands rather than another forced guess.
- **Bad (domain expert would flag):** “help with leave balance or time off,” “set my out-of-office,” a bare colleague's name, or a request with no matching published app produces a form link based on a single shared keyword or on stale single-turn context.
- **Stakes:** High.
- **Source:** `03-CONTEXT.md` D-48–D-55; `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` RTE-08; den Hengst et al., *Conformal Intent Classification and Clarification* (NAACL Findings 2024); Qian et al. on in-domain out-of-scope requests.

**Dimension: Same meaning in English and Vietnamese**
- **Good (domain expert would accept):** Equivalent seeded leave intents (“request leave” / “xin nghỉ phép”) reach the same authorized app; a mixed request such as “không xin nghỉ, cần laptop support” respects the negation and does not open leave. Conflicting mixed-language intents ask a short bilingual clarification; other languages receive bilingual guidance, not a guessed route.
- **Bad (domain expert would flag):** The matcher drops Vietnamese words, treats “không xin nghỉ” as an affirmative leave request, or always prioritizes an English keyword over a contradictory Vietnamese phrase.
- **Stakes:** High.
- **Source:** `03-CONTEXT.md` D-60–D-63; research on code-switched task-oriented intent failures (arXiv:2109.14350). These studies do **not** establish an English/Vietnamese performance figure for this deployment.

**Dimension: Private, dependable handoff**
- **Good (domain expert would accept):** With no approved hosted provider, a clear published-app request can still obtain the same link via local-only routing inside the chat budget, while an unclear one receives useful authorized guidance. Request text, recent turns and employee identifiers do not appear in logs or traces; no employee text goes to a hosted provider without the deployment-region, redaction and no-training/no-retention approvals.
- **Bad (domain expert would flag):** Hosted-provider unavailability produces an infrastructure error instead of local routing, or an email/employee ID from a message or earlier turn is exported to an unapproved provider or logged while diagnosing a failed route.
- **Stakes:** Critical for disclosure; High for failed handoff.
- **Source:** `03-CONTEXT.md` D-54–D-59; `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` FND-06/RTE-05/RTE-06; GDPR Articles 5(1)(c), 28 and Chapter V where applicable; OWASP LLM02:2025 Sensitive Information Disclosure.

### Known Failure Modes in This Domain

1. **Near-neighbor workflow confusion:** Shared words in leave, absence notifications, IT support and purchasing make an unsupported or overlapping task look like a supported app; a wrong form link can misdirect later submissions. Enterprise out-of-scope research specifically finds in-domain unsupported requests hard to distinguish from supported intents (Qian et al.).
2. **Clarification drift and name confusion:** Replaying a cached single-turn decision in another Slack thread, reading “1” without its original choices, interpreting a colleague's homonymous name as the requester, or retaining a choice after role revocation yields a stale or unauthorized destination (`03-CONTEXT.md` D-49/D-50; ACL-06).
3. **Mixed-language and negation loss:** A simple phrase match reads “không xin nghỉ” as leave, or ignores the Vietnamese half of an English/Vietnamese request; code-switched intent research demonstrates this class of failure, but does not measure this project's language pair (arXiv:2109.14350).
4. **Sensitive conversation spillover:** Incident, leave or personnel details embedded in free text or earlier turns evade simple identifier redaction and reach an unapproved hosted endpoint, log or trace; a region-approved host alone does not satisfy this deployment's no-training/no-retention rule (`03-CONTEXT.md` D-56–D-59; OWASP LLM02).

### Regulatory / Compliance Context

Where GDPR applies to employee-message processing, data-minimisation obligations (Article 5(1)(c)), processor-contract obligations (Article 28) and, **if** personal data are transferred to a third country, Chapter V safeguards are relevant; whether a particular deployment is covered and its legal basis require the enterprise privacy owner/counsel to confirm. GDPR is not a blanket legal requirement to host in one region. This project's **additional** requirements are locked-region approval, verified no-training/no-retention for any future hosted provider, local-only operation when none is approved, opaque IDs, PII-free allowlisted logging/tracing, and the narrowed erasure/audit-retention ruling in `.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` §Resolved Requirement Conflicts. The project's three-year audit minimum is not presented as a general GDPR retention period.

## 2. Framework Decision

**Selected Framework:** None. Use the existing frozen `JevProvider` v1.0 seam with Node 24 native `fetch` only for *future approved hosted adapters*, Zod validation at the boundary, and a local `RuleBasedProvider` for the Phase 3 baseline.

**Version:** JEV wire `1.0` (frozen in Phase 1); TypeScript `6.0.3`, Node 24 LTS; no AI-framework package/version to pin.

**Rationale:** This is bounded tool selection, not agent orchestration. The existing versioned request/response schemas, provider interface, NestJS DI and offline terminal fallback already solve the actual problem. An orchestration framework adds state, latency, dependencies and opportunities to bypass authorization without supplying a needed capability. The user explicitly selected a **local-only baseline** because no hosted vendor, model, region and data-use agreement has been approved. Hosted API integration is an optional config-gated extension of the same contract, not a prerequisite for successful offline routing.

**Alternatives Considered:**

| Framework | Ruled Out Because |
|-----------|------------------|
| LangChain.js | Extra abstractions/dependencies for one classified tool choice. May be considered *inside* future approved hosted adapters only if several provider APIs make direct `fetch` burdensome; never owns authz, deadlines, cache or the JEV wire. |
| LangGraph.js | Stateful graph/checkpointing unnecessary for a single permission-filtered choice with bounded clarification; cannot replace the existing Redis/session and worker boundaries. |
| OpenAI Agents SDK | Agent semantics and vendor coupling conflict with config-swappable, region-approved providers and a permanent local-only mode. |

**Vendor Lock-In Accepted:** No. **Hosted model/provider:** none approved or selected for this baseline. No network calls, hosted credentials or token spend in the initial mode; research may specify a vendor-neutral extension contract but must not imply an approved region or provider.

**Contract-consistency handoff:** `.planning/phases/01-foundations-platform/01-CONTEXT.md` D-23 calls `choice.verified: false` a confident choice, but the delivered `packages/contract/src/jev/v1/jev-response.ts` describes `true` as verified and `packages/contract/src/jev/jev-v1.frozen.spec.ts` uses `verified: true` for the successful choice. The response schema only enforces the `null`-abstention rule. This spec follows the **tested implementation** and never issues a link for `false`; Phase 3 planning must explicitly reconcile the conflicting D-23 prose and add a negative test for `false` before integrating any provider. Do not silently change the v1 wire shape.

### Entry Point Pattern
The following self-contained TypeScript **demo** module can be placed inside `packages/domain/src/decision/` and compiled with `npm run build`; run/import it only for an isolated smoke test, then separate the provider from the demo invocation before production wiring. The map is **illustrative published-app declaration data**, not a new central intent catalogue; production must load aliases/descriptions only from published, currently authorized apps and use calibrated, per-app thresholds in the orchestrator. This minimal exact-phrase scorer is not an accuracy guarantee.

```ts
import {
  JEV_SPEC_VERSION, JevRequestSchema, JevResponseSchema,
  RULE_BASED_PROVIDER_NAME,
  type JevCapability, type JevProvider, type JevRequest, type JevResponse,
} from '@akane/contract';

const normalized = (text: string): string =>
  ` ${text.normalize('NFC').toLowerCase().trim().replace(/\s+/gu, ' ')} `;

class RuleBasedProvider implements JevProvider {
  readonly name = RULE_BASED_PROVIDER_NAME;
  readonly spec_version = JEV_SPEC_VERSION;
  readonly capabilities: JevCapability[] = ['tool_selection', 'clarification', 'multi_turn'];

  constructor(private readonly declaredAliases: ReadonlyMap<string, readonly string[]>) {}

  async decide(input: JevRequest): Promise<JevResponse> {
    const started = performance.now();
    const req = JevRequestSchema.parse(input);
    // The caller has already filtered req.tools through current per-app RBAC.
    const words = normalized([
      ...req.context.previous_turns.filter(turn => turn.role === 'user').map(turn => turn.text),
      req.question,
    ].join(' '));
    // Minimal fail-closed negation handling: do not turn 'not request leave' into a link.
    const negated = /(?:^|[^\p{L}])(?:not|don't|không|đừng)(?=$|[^\p{L}])/iu.test(words);
    const matches = req.state.force_clarification || negated ||
      !['en', 'vi'].includes(req.state.locale)
      ? []
      : req.tools.filter(tool =>
          (this.declaredAliases.get(tool.id) ?? []).some(alias =>
            words.includes(normalized(alias))));
    const sole = matches.length === 1 ? matches[0] : undefined;
    const response: JevResponse = {
      spec_version: JEV_SPEC_VERSION,
      request_id: req.request_id,
      choice: sole
        ? { tool_id: sole.id, confidence: 1, verified: true }
        : { tool_id: null, confidence: 0, verified: null },
      ...(matches.length > 1 ? {
        alternatives: matches.slice(0, 3).map(tool => ({ tool_id: tool.id, confidence: 1 })),
      } : {}),
      clarification_needed: !sole,
      ...(!sole ? { clarification_prompt: 'Which authorized app did you mean?' } : {}),
      provider: this.name,
      latency_ms: performance.now() - started,
    };
    return JevResponseSchema.parse(response);
  }

  async healthCheck() {
    return {
      status: 'healthy' as const,
      model_version: 'rules-v1',
      spec_version: this.spec_version,
      capabilities: this.capabilities,
      uptime_seconds: process.uptime(),
    };
  }
}

// Demo seed from a published app's bilingual declarations; no API request is made.
const local = new RuleBasedProvider(new Map([
  ['hr-leave-request:create_leave', ['request leave', 'xin nghỉ phép']],
]));
const req = JevRequestSchema.parse({
  spec_version: JEV_SPEC_VERSION,
  request_id: 'demo-1',
  state: { locale: 'en', conversation_id: 'thread-1', turn_count: 0, force_clarification: false },
  question: 'I want to request leave',
  context: { real_user_id: 'opaque-1', app_hints: [], previous_turns: [] },
  tools: [{ id: 'hr-leave-request:create_leave', description: 'Leave request', parameters: [] }],
});
void local.decide(req).then(result => {
  if (result.choice.verified !== true) throw new Error('Expected an exact local match');
});
```

### Key Abstractions
| Concept | What It Is | When You Use It |
|---------|-----------|-----------------|
| `JevProvider` | Frozen async `decide(req)` / `healthCheck()` seam and capability list | DI-registered, config-ordered providers; local provider always terminal |
| `JevRequestSchema` | Strict v1 request including filtered `tools`, opaque user ID and clarification hint | Validate after current RBAC filtering, before any provider or cache lookup |
| `JevResponseSchema` | Strict response; required `choice`, `verified: null` means *total* abstention | Validate every local, hosted or cached result before considering a link |
| `RuleBasedProvider` | Offline scorer of published English/Vietnamese intent declarations | Baseline and terminal fallback; handles empty/conflicting/unknown as clarification |
| Deadline + capability gate | Caller-owned 2-second budget and `multi_turn`/`clarification` checks | Before invoking an eligible provider; reserve time for local match and reply |

### Common Pitfalls
1. **Trusting a provider or cache to enforce permissions:** `tools` must already be RBAC-filtered; check the chosen ID *and every displayed alternative* against the current set and re-authorize before signing a link.
2. **Treating a TypeScript cast as validation:** a provider can omit required `choice`, add fields or return inconsistent abstention; use `safeParse` and compare `spec_version`/`request_id`, not `as JevResponse`.
3. **Conflating `verified: null` with low confidence or letting `confidence: 1` imply certainty:** null always clarifies; local exact match is a deterministic score, not a calibrated probability, and the app's configured threshold still gates issuance.
4. **Putting the whole catalogue or old chat turns in the request:** unauthorized descriptions leak, and stale clarification context picks the wrong app; use only bounded same-thread turns plus current authorized published declarations.
5. **Assuming `AbortSignal.timeout(2000)` preserves fallback time:** it spends the *whole* chat budget and does not change `JevProvider.decide(req)`'s signature; future HTTP adapters must use a shorter remaining-budget timeout, cancel their own fetch, and leave a local/reply reserve.

## 4. Implementation Guidance

**Model Configuration:** Production baseline is `RuleBasedProvider` only: **no LLM, model identifier, temperature, token limit, embeddings or hosted endpoint**. Version the English/Vietnamese matcher and published-app declarations; app-specific acceptance thresholds must be measured in the 50-intent suite, not copied from PRD examples. No network dependency or credential is needed for a valid local-only deployment. Future hosted model/parameters remain **unselected** pending locked-region approval, no-training/no-retention proof, data redaction review and conformance; a config flag alone cannot grant these approvals.

**Core Pattern:** Place routing in the inbound **worker**, after identity and fresh RBAC filtering, before the existing Phase 2 link issuer. The following TypeScript is an integration pattern, not a new contract or permission service; the caller supplies only published, currently authorized tool descriptors and the existing per-app RBAC checker. Never call this on webhook ingest.

```ts
import {
  JevRequestSchema, JevResponseSchema,
  type JevProvider, type JevRequest, type ToolDescriptor,
} from '@akane/contract';

type Outcome = { kind: 'link'; toolId: string } | { kind: 'clarify'; toolIds: string[] };

async function routeLocal(
  draft: Omit<JevRequest, 'tools'>,
  authorizedTools: ToolDescriptor[], // produced from fresh per-app RBAC, NOT global catalogue
  local: JevProvider,
  threshold: (toolId: string) => number, // app's configured, measured threshold
  canOpenNow: (toolId: string) => Promise<boolean>, // re-check current permission
  deadlineEpochMs: number,
): Promise<Outcome> {
  const clarify = (): Outcome => ({ kind: 'clarify', toolIds: [] });
  const permitted = new Set(authorizedTools.map(tool => tool.id));
  const parsedRequest = JevRequestSchema.safeParse({
    ...draft,
    context: { ...draft.context, app_hints: draft.context.app_hints.filter(id => permitted.has(id)) },
    tools: authorizedTools,
  });
  if (!parsedRequest.success) return clarify(); // do not log rejected fields or raw text
  const req = parsedRequest.data;
  if (req.state.force_clarification || req.tools.length === 0 ||
      Date.now() >= deadlineEpochMs) return clarify();

  let decision: unknown;
  try {
    // Only the offline provider is configured in the initial deployment.
    decision = await local.decide(req);
  } catch {
    return clarify(); // local failure never turns into an unauthorized link
  }
  const parsed = JevResponseSchema.safeParse(decision);
  if (!parsed.success || parsed.data.spec_version !== req.spec_version ||
      parsed.data.request_id !== req.request_id || Date.now() >= deadlineEpochMs)
    return clarify();

  const result = parsed.data;
  const id = result.choice.tool_id;
  const gate = id !== null && permitted.has(id) ? threshold(id) : Number.NaN;
  // Total abstention, low/false verification and ambiguity always clarify.
  if (result.choice.verified === true && !result.clarification_needed && id !== null &&
      permitted.has(id) && Number.isFinite(result.choice.confidence) &&
      result.choice.confidence >= 0 && result.choice.confidence <= 1 &&
      Number.isFinite(gate) && gate >= 0 && gate <= 1 &&
      result.choice.confidence >= gate &&
      !(result.alternatives ?? []).some(alt => permitted.has(alt.tool_id)) &&
      await canOpenNow(id) && Date.now() < deadlineEpochMs) {
    return { kind: 'link', toolId: id }; // link issuer uses Phase 2 signed-link path
  }
  const toolIds: string[] = [];
  for (const alt of result.alternatives ?? []) {
    if (Date.now() >= deadlineEpochMs) return clarify();
    if (toolIds.length === 3) break;
    if (permitted.has(alt.tool_id) && await canOpenNow(alt.tool_id))
      toolIds.push(alt.tool_id);
  }
  return Date.now() >= deadlineEpochMs ? clarify() : { kind: 'clarify', toolIds };
}
```

Set the **shared** 2-second chat-to-link deadline at IM receipt and propagate its absolute expiration through queue and worker (including identity, RBAC, local match, recheck, link and reply); on expiration send a safe clarification/help response, never a link. Future approved adapters are optional ordered links before the mandatory local provider: require capability compatibility (`multi_turn` for same-thread follow-ups), per-provider health sampled every 60 seconds, a timeout **shorter than remaining budget**, and a reserve for local match/reply. Because `JevProvider.decide(req)` has **no signal argument**, the future HTTP adapter must own its `fetch(..., { signal: AbortSignal.timeout(adapterBudgetMs) })` and abort body reading too; `fetch` does not throw just because HTTP status is 4xx/5xx, so check `response.ok`, bound response size and validate with Zod before routing. Redact the current question and every included previous turn and apply regional/no-retention gates **before** any outbound request; uncertain free-text redaction means skip the hosted provider. Never mutate the frozen JEV fields or send profile/submission data.

**Tool Use:** `tools` means app descriptors, **not** arbitrary executable LLM tools. Filter published apps by live per-app RBAC and pass only their permitted `id`, `description`, `parameters`; fetch app-owner intents/aliases locally. A provider never calls the downstream app; after selection, recheck permission at the execution boundary and issue through Phase 2. No vector DB, embedding model, AI tracing service or new eval dependency is required: use existing OpenTelemetry with the frozen field allowlist and Vitest for conformance.

**State Management:** Persist at most the original request, up to two clarification replies, displayed **authorized** choice IDs/names and expiry in the existing Redis session path keyed to the same Slack thread/opaque user; no process-local conversation memory or sticky sessions. Re-filter permissions on **every** reply. Choosing a displayed name/number must recheck current authorization; added details re-evaluate original request + reply, never as a new context-free turn. Render clarification from worker-owned localized templates (English, Vietnamese, or concise bilingual for mixed-language ambiguity), never relay an untrusted provider's raw `clarification_prompt` directly. After two failed replies, offer authorized examples/slash commands and stop.

**Context Window Strategy:** Offline matching has no model window. Bound input length and previous turns, use only the original same-thread request plus at most two clarification replies, and treat English/Vietnamese phrases independently so mixed-language conflicts clarify. Unsupported languages set `force_clarification` and get concise bilingual guidance. For any future approved model, fit **only RBAC-filtered** compact descriptors and these bounded turns under a measured token budget; do not silently drop authorized candidates and claim global accuracy at 100–800 tools. If the permitted candidate set cannot fit, use an evaluated local shortlist with safe clarification rather than truncating without notice.

### 4b.1 Structured Outputs with Pydantic

**Production:** `JevRequestSchema.safeParse()` before invoking a provider; `JevResponseSchema.safeParse()` after every provider/cache result; then verify request ID, version, permitted tool IDs, tri-state abstention, configured threshold and fresh RBAC before a link. TypeScript interfaces alone do not validate JSON. The *only* authoritative shape is `packages/contract/src/jev/v1/`. Do not use an LLM structured-output library in local mode.

**NON-PRODUCTION compatibility illustration, hypothetical Python provider only (Pydantic v2):** This mirrors relevant v1 output fields; keep it in a separate future adapter test, not in the Node workspace. If Python were ever approved, compare serialization against the published v1 JSON Schema and still run the TypeScript Zod gate.

```python
from typing import Literal
from pydantic import BaseModel, ConfigDict, model_validator

class Choice(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra='forbid', strict=True)
    tool_id: str | None
    confidence: float
    verified: bool | None  # None means abstention, never an implicit choice

class Alternative(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra='forbid', strict=True)
    tool_id: str
    confidence: float

class JevResponseCompat(BaseModel):
    model_config = ConfigDict(extra='forbid', strict=True)
    spec_version: Literal['1.0']
    request_id: str
    choice: Choice
    alternatives: list[Alternative] | None = None
    clarification_needed: bool
    clarification_prompt: str | None = None
    provider: str
    latency_ms: float

    @model_validator(mode='after')
    def total_abstention(self) -> 'JevResponseCompat':
        if self.choice.verified is None and (
            self.choice.tool_id is not None or not self.clarification_needed
        ):
            raise ValueError('inconsistent abstention')
        return self

def validate_hypothetical_json(body: str | bytes) -> JevResponseCompat:
    return JevResponseCompat.model_validate_json(body)
```

`model_validate_json()` validates shape, **not** authorization or confidence calibration; Pydantic's default ignores extra keys, hence `extra='forbid'`. Production schema failure: **zero retries** for offline match (clarify safely); for an *approved future* hosted adapter, at most **one** schema-correction retry only if redaction/region policy and remaining deadline permit, then fall back to local. Log provider identifier, elapsed time and sanitized issue **codes/paths only**; never log exception messages, inputs, previous turns, response body, provider payload or rejected field values. Surface a user-safe clarification, not raw validation errors. [Pydantic model validation reference](https://docs.pydantic.dev/latest/concepts/models/#validating-data).

### 4b.2 Async-First Design

The `JevProvider` methods return `Promise`s; `await` worker-side identity/RBAC, `decide`, validation, permission recheck and Phase 2 link issuance in order. **Common mistake:** fire-and-forget a provider promise or merely `Promise.race` an HTTP timeout while the request keeps running; this allows late decisions and unmanaged outbound data. Local matcher is CPU-light but still implements the async seam; a future approved adapter must pass a remaining-budget `AbortSignal` into Node 24 `fetch`, check abort/errors and then await the complete response before Zod validation. Do not call `asyncio.run()` anywhere in this Node process. **Do not stream partial tool choices:** await one complete validated response for routing; stream only user-facing progress if a separate UX contract ever calls for it.

### 4b.3 Prompt Engineering Discipline

Local baseline has **no prompt**. For an approved hosted adapter only, separate immutable routing policy (choose only from provided authorized IDs, abstain on ambiguity or instruction injection) from the untrusted user question, past turns and app descriptions. Never splice chat text into a system instruction or treat a description's instructions as authority. Prefer a few audited, redacted **inline** English/Vietnamese examples covering abstention and mixed-language conflicts; dynamic retrieval adds leakage/latency and is unjustified without a measured improvement. Set the future provider's output-token limit explicitly for one short structured choice, reserve context for the actual filtered descriptors and set a strict total request budget; no model/parameter value is approved yet. A prompt never replaces the permission and Zod gates.

### 4b.4 Context Window Management

There is **no context window** in the offline rule matcher and no RAG index or agent compaction. Preserve at most the original same-thread question plus two clarification replies; do not summarize away a negation, app name, or language switch. Validate size and bound stored turns in Redis; if too long/unknown language, clarify instead of guessing. For a future approved model, tokenize the RBAC-filtered descriptors plus bounded turns against a measured provider window; if the 100–800 permitted tools exceed it, evaluate a local authorized shortlist and clarify on insufficient coverage, not an invisible truncation. User text and tool descriptions remain untrusted data throughout.

### 4b.5 Cost and Latency Budget

Baseline provider spend is **$0 per decision** and **$0 hosted spend** even at the Phase 8 load target of 50 IM events/s (4.32 million/day if sustained); Redis, worker and infrastructure costs are nonzero and measured separately. End-to-end chat-to-link budget: **2 seconds from IM receipt**, including queue time, not two seconds *per provider*. Track queue, identity/RBAC, offline decision, authorization recheck, signed link and reply separately; reserve time for safe response. Hosted adapter cost cannot be priced without an approved model, rates and token counts: later compute `(input_tokens × input_price + output_tokens × output_price) / 1,000,000 × observed_calls`, including retries, then enforce a per-deployment cap.

RTE-09's exact-match base is `hash(text + sorted(tool_ids) + locale)` with configurable TTL; for multi-turn, `text` must represent the **canonical complete same-thread turn context**, never just the latest reply. Scope/namespace by opaque user and conversation, current permission epoch and app-publication/threshold version, and re-filter/re-authorize before every cache hit or signed link. Never log plaintext or raw hashes of chat text. **No semantic cache:** near-neighbor matches could conflate users, negations or clarification contexts; no embeddings or cheap sub-task model is needed for bounded local classification. If hosted routing is approved later, price and evaluate a cheaper classifier only after proving the same authorization, abstention and regional controls.

## 5. Evaluation Strategy

Evaluate the **worker's authorized routing outcome**, not a generic model benchmark. The initial system is a bounded, local-only classifier (Section 1); there is no approved LLM or judge. The hard failure modes are unauthorized disclosure/link, incorrect forced routing, unsafe stale follow-up/cache, outbound/text disclosure, and missed shared deadline (Section 1; `03-CONTEXT.md` D-48–D-63). An app's configured confidence threshold is a decision gate, **not** proof that its provider score is a calibrated probability. Slash commands are a separate deterministic control.

### Dimensions

| Dimension | Rubric (Pass/Fail) | Measurement Approach | Priority |
|-----------|--------------------|----------------------|----------|
| Department boundary / safety | **PASS:** Only current published, per-app-authorized tools reach *any* provider; choice, up to three displayed alternatives, and issued link remain authorized after a fresh check, including revocation and homonymous names. **FAIL:** A restricted HR app reaches the provider, appears in a numbered choice, or opens after a permission change, even if the provider would not select it. | **Code:** spy on provider request; inspect all outbound IDs, cache hit, reply, link and revoked-epoch cases using real Redis/RBAC integration fixtures; zero exposure events. **Human:** security owner reviews adversarial fixtures. | Critical |
| Correct service-desk destination / task completion | **PASS:** Clear, published, permitted “request leave” / “xin nghỉ phép” reaches the same signed leave-form path as `/leave request`; laptop connectivity routes to an IT incident, not a purchase. **FAIL:** A wrong app receives a link or an unambiguous supported request is silently dropped. | **Code:** gold expected app ID + link-or-clarify outcome, slash parity E2E; report per-app precision and recall with intervals. **Human:** HR/IT/procurement owner labels overlaps. | High |
| Abstention and useful clarification | **PASS:** Unknown/in-domain out-of-scope, weak, conflicting, negative or unsupported-language input returns no link and a concise appropriate question/guidance containing at most three *currently permitted* choices; `verified: null` always abstains; two unsuccessful replies end in examples/slash guidance. **FAIL:** “không xin nghỉ, cần laptop support”, “out-of-office message”, bare colleague name or no available app forces leave, or a third clarification loop starts. | **Code:** labelled outcome and copy-shape assertions; threshold boundary, `force_clarification`, empty toolset and loop-limit tests. **Human:** bilingual service-desk review of whether questions are understandable. | High |
| English/Vietnamese parity | **PASS:** Parallel seeded English and Vietnamese leave/IT intents reach the same authorized service; mixed-language negation is respected, mixed competing intents get bilingual clarification, other languages get bilingual guidance. **FAIL:** Vietnamese tokens are ignored or an English keyword overrides a Vietnamese negation. | **Code:** paired multilingual fixtures, language-bucket confusion/abstention reports; **Human:** bilingual reviewer labels and audits ambiguous phrasing. | High |
| Same-thread context and permission freshness | **PASS:** “1”/published app name resolves only against its original same-thread displayed choices and fresh RBAC; added details re-evaluate original request plus reply; a new thread or changed role cannot reuse a single-turn answer. **FAIL:** Context-free “1”, another employee/thread or stale cache opens a form. | **Code:** Vitest + Redis/testcontainers replay, different user/thread, turn order, expiry, epoch bump with dropped Pub/Sub, changed publication/threshold and multi-turn cache-key collision tests. | Critical |
| Wire/decision validity | **PASS:** Every provider and cache result passes frozen strict `JevRequestSchema` / `JevResponseSchema`, matching v1 `spec_version` and `request_id`; null verification has null tool and requests clarification; low/unverified responses cannot issue links; all suggested IDs are checked. **FAIL:** malformed response, mismatched ID/version, unpermitted alternative or fabricated confidence is treated as a route. | **Code:** schema/property and negative contract tests (including malformed request) against frozen JEV v1; test threshold immediately below/at each configured app gate. | Critical |
| Private, dependable handoff | **PASS:** With hosted disabled or simulated down, clear local requests still get the signed form link within **2 s from IM receipt**, while unsafe ones safely clarify; outbound calls remain zero unless region, redaction and no-training/no-retention are approved; routing logs/traces contain no text, user identifiers or submission IDs. **FAIL:** hosted outage surfaces an error or eats the fallback reserve, text leaks through a log/trace/event, or a future unapproved host receives a request. | **Code:** clock/timeout/HTTP-spy tests across queue→reply, redaction in current/prior turns, exporter/log capture with sentinel strings and provider gate; report observed end-to-end latency and budget misses separately from provider latency. **Human:** privacy owner reviews future host approval, not a model judge. | Critical |

### Reference Dataset

**Size and timeline:** Start with 10–20 reviewed synthetic/approved examples **while implementing** the local matcher; reach **at least 50 distinct, labelled intents** for the per-provider RTE-11 conformance gate before release (production minimum is therefore already exceeded). Keep independent negative contract and timing cases outside this count. Grow a *separate*, balanced held-out set before claiming accuracy across larger catalogues; 50 conformance cases cannot establish accuracy for 100–800 candidates or every app/language subgroup. Version fixture labels and published-app declaration snapshots; avoid using raw employee conversations in CI.

**Composition and oracle:** Include clear HR leave, IT incident and procurement destinations; near-neighbor HR absence/out-of-office and laptop incident/purchase collisions; in-domain unsupported, unknown, empty list, malformed request, negation, adversarial quoted app names and unapproved language. Allocate the initial 50+ across English, Vietnamese, mixed/code-switched and ambiguous/unsupported buckets; pair equivalent en/vi requests and include both correct-route and must-clarify labels. For each case record synthetic `question`, same-thread history (if any), published candidate snapshot, permission snapshot, locale, expected app ID **or** expected clarification, and expected authorized displayed IDs. Independently exercise 0, 1–3, 4–20, 21–99 and **100–800 authorized-candidate** buckets with generated *published/permitted* toolsets and adversarial overlapping descriptors; report fixture counts per bucket, not pretend the 50 covers all cells. Include outage, deadline, revocation, new-thread and altered catalogue cases in separate integration/stress sets. Never put identifiable case text into exported telemetry.

**Labeling and measurement:** HR, IT/procurement app owners and a bilingual English/Vietnamese reviewer independently label their own clear/ambiguous cases; resolve disagreements with the security owner for permission boundaries. Privacy owner signs off future hosted examples before any external use. Use code for expected-ID, outcome and schema checks; human review for semantic overlap and bilingual copy. An LLM judge is **not** part of the local-only gate; consider one only after an approved, region-bound, calibrated judge agrees with human labels on a held-out set (per `ai-evals.md`, target correlation ≥0.7), and never for authorization. After conformance, report per app **precision** = correct issued links to app / all links issued to app and **recall** = correct issued links to app / labelled requests for app (abstentions on clear routes count as misses), plus false-link and abstention rates; show numerators, denominators and two-sided **95% Wilson intervals** for each applicable binomial proportion. Report en/vi/mixed/ambiguous and candidate-count strata separately with interval widths; label zero-denominator cells N/A and small/wide intervals inconclusive. Calibrate each app threshold on held-out owner-labelled cases and sign off its observed trade-off/interval precision before release; no shared numerical accuracy target or claim of high-candidate-scale accuracy is justified without adequate denominators and a measured baseline. Retest after any matcher, threshold, app declaration or provider change (`APP-07` will add its separate publication-share gate in Phase 6).

## 6. Guardrails

Only catastrophic controls run per request; semantic quality is measured offline, not delegated to an extra online model. Tests inject violations at **each** boundary (provider, cache, clarification and signed-link issuer).

### Online (Real-Time)

| Guardrail | Trigger | Intervention |
|-----------|---------|--------------|
| Authorization before *any* provider/cache + after choice | On every message and reply: resolve authenticated real user, load published apps and current per-app RBAC/epoch, filter tools and app hints **before** deciding; recheck chosen and displayed IDs immediately before link/choice. | **Block** disallowed IDs; never send unauthorized descriptor to local or future hosted provider, never name one in a reply; safely clarify or deny if access changed (ACL-06/07/08; RTE-03). Do not trust a previously displayed name/number. |
| Strict input/output and confidence gate | Malformed v1 request, empty candidates, bad/mismatched provider or cached response, `verified !== true`, `verified: null`, forced clarification, weak score below the *chosen app's* configured threshold or unknown ID. | **Abstain/clarify**, no link; only signed Phase 2 issuer can create an authorized link. Do not treat `confidence: 1` as calibrated certainty or relay provider-generated prompt text (RTE-08/10/11). |
| Conversation/cache isolation | Every clarification reply; wrong Slack thread/user, stale permission epoch, changed publication/threshold version, expired context or a single-turn hash applied to a different multi-turn history. | **Invalidate/ignore** stale decision, re-filter current tools and re-evaluate bounded original request + reply; allow at most two replies, then send authorized guidance. RTE-09's `hash(text + sorted(tool_ids) + locale)` remains the base; canonical complete same-thread text and scope/version prevent cross-context reuse (D-49–D-51). |
| Hosted data boundary (future only) | Before registration **and before each outbound call**: no approved region/processor no-training/no-retention proof, unverified current/prior-turn redaction, sensitive free text, or insufficient remaining budget. | **Skip hosted call and use local**; don't send employee text/profile/submissions to a non-approved endpoint. No hosted adapter is enabled in baseline. Host approval is an external governance decision, not a config flag (D-56–D-59). |
| Shared deadline and outage | Absolute **IM-receipt + 2 s** deadline approaches/expires; hosted attempt times out/unhealthy (60 s health probes) or returns malformed data. | **Abort** adapter-owned HTTP attempt while preserving local/reply reserve; continue to local provider; after deadline send safe help/clarification, never a late link or infrastructure error. No provider gets an independent 2 s budget (RTE-05–RTE-07). |
| Telemetry minimization | Routing span/log emission, including errors/exception events and automatically instrumented HTTP/queue fields. | Export only existing allowlisted, non-personal **routing-stage** fields and fixed code values; never put request/previous-turn text, generated prompts, user/employee ID, submission ID, payload, raw URL or text hash in new routing logs/spans/metrics, even if an older generic log allowlist permits an opaque ID. Block unsafe recording at call sites and test exported bytes (FND-06/07). |

### Offline (Flywheel)

| Metric | Sampling Strategy | Action on Degradation |
|--------|------------------|----------------------|
| Per-app misroute, abstention and threshold behavior, with 95% Wilson intervals | Replay versioned labelled suite and balanced held-out fixtures after matcher/app/threshold change; enrich with approved human-labelled problem cases, not raw telemetry. | HR/IT owners inspect false links, near-neighbor collisions and small/wide intervals; adjust per-app aliases/thresholds and rerun all providers before promotion. |
| Language and catalogue-size coverage | Stratify en/vi/mixed/ambiguous and 0/1–3/4–20/21–99/100–800 authorized tools; weight reviews toward negative/mixed/high-candidate bins. | Bilingual reviewers add equivalent/negation pairs; expand independent held-out high-candidate sets until intervals can support claims; clarify rather than silently truncate. |
| Clarification quality and follow-up completion | Aggregate enum-only clarify→choice, repeated-reply and slash fallback counts; approved human review of a narrowly accessed, separately redacted case if needed. | Improve worker-owned localized templates and disambiguation fixtures; investigate user retries despite apparently good offline accuracy (signal/metric divergence). |
| Fallback and cache correctness | Offline inject hosted 429/timeouts, cancelled calls, expired deadline, dropped invalidation Pub/Sub, changed permissions and new thread; review aggregate fallback/cache-hit error codes in production. | Fix reserve/caching or disable unsafe cache/provider; replay revocation and no-host tests before re-enable. No quality metric excuses an online boundary breach. |

## 7. Production Monitoring

**Tracing tool:** The **existing** vendor-neutral OpenTelemetry bootstrap (`apps/*/otel.mjs`, `packages/platform/src/otel/otel.bootstrap.ts`) with `AllowlistSpanExporter` → approved in-region OTLP collector/trace backend and the existing single OTel meter → `/metrics`. No Phoenix, hosted eval platform or second tracing pipeline is needed. Span **names** (fixed constants, never chat text) identify IM receive, identity, RBAC, decision, link, form, submit, route and respond for `OBS-02`; queue-to-worker trace propagation must preserve the chain. The export allowlist filters attributes, **not automatically names, events, exception messages, resource attributes or log `msg`**: keep each of those fixed/sanitized as well, and negative-test exported span/log bytes with sentinel PII/text. Existing `app_id` is an opaque app scope, never a person or `submission_id`; do not add any identifier, question, prior turn, prompt, hash or raw URL to routing telemetry (`.planning/REQUIREMENTS.md` FND-06/07; `packages/platform/src/otel/span-attribute-allowlist.ts`). Human case review uses a separate approved restricted/redacted channel, **not** trace/log content.

**Key metrics (new routing instruments planned on the existing OTel meter; none claimed already implemented):**
1. `routing_decisions_total` by **bounded** provider (`local` or approved host), outcome (`link`/`clarify`/`deny`/`fallback`), locale bucket and tool-count bucket; track empty list, schema failure, unauthorized-attempt, deadline and cache-stale as closed reason codes, **never** raw text/user or conversation IDs. Alertable counts come from the orchestrator, not solely provider self-reports.
2. `routing_end_to_end_duration_ms` histogram **from IM receipt including queue time through reply/link**, plus separate queue/RBAC/provider/local/recheck/reply timing. Report p50/p95/p99 and fraction exceeding 2 s by local-only versus fallback; provider `latency_ms` alone does not establish success (Roadmap Phase 3 SC3).
3. `routing_provider_health` / `routing_fallback_total` and the 60-second probe age for local and future approved providers; no-host baseline should show zero outbound calls and local readiness.
4. `routing_clarification_total`, `routing_permission_denied_total` and `routing_cache_stale_total` by closed reason; track clarification exhaustion/retries and revocation independently of app selection. Do not infer accuracy from unlabelled traffic.
5. `routing_share{app_id}` from issued **authorized** links (opaque app IDs only) plus owner-labelled offline per-app precision/recall with denominators and Wilson intervals. Detect share drift/new app collisions, **not** a global “decision accuracy” percentage (`OBS-05` future dashboard requirement).

**Alert thresholds / actions:**
- **Immediate security incident / page on any confirmed event**: unauthorized ID sent to *any* provider, shown in choices or issued a link; unapproved hosted egress; routing text/user/submission identifier exported in logs/traces. Disable offending provider/cache and investigate RBAC/privacy breach; zero tolerance is a safety invariant, not a statistical target.
- **Page when the local provider is unhealthy or all configured eligible providers are unavailable**; no-host is a valid configuration and hosted-only outage with healthy local fallback is **not** an availability page. Probe provider health every 60 s (RTE-07). Alert separately when hosted timeouts repeatedly consume the local reserve, or when a 2-second end-to-end budget miss persists over a 5-minute window; preserve counts and sample sizes. The **2 s limit is the requirement**, while the volume needed for paging vs investigating is calibrated on the load/traffic baseline rather than invented here.
- **Investigate quality**, not automatically page, on a sustained increase in wrong-link, abstention, language disparity, high-candidate misses or routing-share drift relative to a labelled baseline with intervals and adequate denominators. `OBS-06`'s later average-confidence <0.6 over 10 minutes is an operations alert requirement, **not an accuracy threshold**: a low confidence score is neither a wrong decision nor calibrated probability; corroborate with labelled outcomes before modifying thresholds. `APP-07`'s >5 percentage-point routing-share publication block belongs to the later publish workflow, not to this phase's aggregate conformance gate.

**Smart sampling strategy:** All requests run the cheap authorization/schema/threshold/deadline/egress guardrails and contribute **aggregated** counters and timing with low-cardinality enum buckets. Trace sampling may be reduced after continuity is verified, but retain error/deadline/fallback traces **only with fixed sanitized names and allowlisted fields**. Weekly offline review samples available *consented and separately redacted* cases (not a reconstruction from logs) toward clarified, repeated, unsupported/mixed-language, timeout/fallback, revocation and 100–800-candidate cohorts, with a smaller random sample for drift; security/privacy owners control access and retention. Compare user follow-up/slash abandonment signals against labelled offline scores and expand the reference set when they diverge. Do not sample raw message bodies into a new observability backend.
